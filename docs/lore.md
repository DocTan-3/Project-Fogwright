# Fogwright Lore

Nov 22, 2026 · @Dev Tan

The world's history in three ages, built from your ideas. Nothing here is final.

**Tone:** the world looks like a medieval high-tech high fantasy, but the story gradually reveals a grimdark reality underneath. Players live in a world that was once peaceful and is now forgotten, and they learn its history slowly, through ruins, relics, writings and stories rather than being told.

## The three ages

*[Diagram: timeline · the three ages and their turning points. It lives in the online doc and does not export to Markdown.]*

The split of Erde ends the first age; the 13th God's sacrifice ends the second and begins the third, when the game is set.

## The first age

The first age is ancient history: an empire of men ruled the whole world, every domain and every place, on the single continent of Erde.

The Gods blessed the empire with everything, on two conditions:

1. Men must never fight among themselves.
2. Men must never think themselves above the gods.

## The corruption and the split

The Gods were not perfect: some sought to be greater, and corrupted men into waging war on their behalf, breaking both rules.

- The empire split apart.
- The head of the Gods, the 1st God, split the continent of Erde itself. He was that strong. The split was a way for the Gods to appease themselves.
- Each god took their own domain, with their own people: humans changed by that god.
- The gods who started the corruption were punished by being confined together in a single large domain, a realm of evil. They kept using their magic.
- A new peace was born.

**The hidden truth:** the Gods never knew that the head of the Gods was the first to be corrupted. His plan was long: wait until the others grew weak.

## The second age

The second age is the age of conquest: peoples advanced their cultures and technology into a light steampunk era, all to fight one another.

- **The ban on magic.** On the word of the head of the Gods, the gods made all the "good" races outlaw magic, to restore the old rules. Only a select few individuals may still use it, each claimed by their own god. These chosen tend to be important in history.
- **Endless war.** Wars kept being fought and the world grew bleak.
- **The birth of the Fog.** Magic mixed with the smoke and fire of all that unrest and became the thick Fog that covered the world.

## The third age

The third age, when the game is set, is the war against the Fog.

- **The sacrifice.** The 13th God, kindest of all, sacrificed herself to weaken the Fog. Her sacrifice began the third age.
- **The fading gods.** Twelve gods remain. Each sits at the heart of a major city, strong enough to keep the Fog out of their own domain, but not much else. They are slowly losing their magic.
- **Her legacy.** The 13th left her own magic behind: a mysterious force that guides chosen individuals to find their gods and be claimed. The players are these chosen.
- **The great evil.** The plot of the third age is the discovery that no gods remain in the evil lands, and that a single great evil has risen there. The fate of the third age hangs in the balance.

## The Gods

There were once many gods; over the ages they dwindled to thirteen, then twelve.

| God | Role | Third age |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The 1st, head of the Gods | Split Erde; ordered the ban on magic | Leads the twelve. Secretly the 14th |
| The 2nd to the 12th | Each rules a domain and its people | Each at the heart of a city, fading |
| The 13th, kindest of all | Sacrificed herself to weaken the Fog | Gone; her magic guides the chosen |
| The 14th | Rose by conquering all the evil gods | Hidden in the evil lands; the great evil |

**The reveal:** the head of the Gods and the 14th are one and the same. He was corrupted first, ordered the ban so the others would weaken, and absorbed the evil gods to rise anew. Players do not know this at the start.

### The lost gods

Four of the twelve stand apart. Oriel and Vael lost their peoples before the third age; those peoples live on only in writings, stories and relics that players can uncover. Verdane never chose a people. Galeth falls during the story.

**Oriel the Lucid and the Animari.** The Animari were exceptionally intelligent humans who could use magic to move their minds into machines for a time. They gave birth to technomancy, Fogwright's answer to necromancy. The greatest technomancers of old may have raised mountains of metal as armies; today the art is crude, archaic and outlawed for its unexplained tie to magic. In play, technomancers summon a few small, special creations rather than armies, and summons can gather resources in crafting.

**Vael the Unseen and the warps.** Vael was thought to be among the first gods to die out. In truth he hid. His people's ancient civilization left behind the warps: portals between places. The great cities grew around them, often with a warp at their heart. Only those chosen by a god can use a warp, and warps link only the great cities, so ships and roads still matter. One class wields warp-style magic. Vael accidentally warped into the place where the 1st God hides his plans. He knows the truth, but chose to hide and abandoned his people; their anger at him lives on in their history.

**Verdane the Open-Handed and nature magic.** Verdane chose no people. Instead she taught her magic to all peoples: weather, growth and the living world.

**Galeth the Vigilant and the Lantern Vigil.** Galeth, god of protection, becomes the first of the remaining gods to be devoured by the Fog, as an event during the story. His crusader-like order of men, fiercely loyal, journeys into the Fog to search for him. Their search is part of the main story.

## Magic

Magic is a mysterious driving force, in the spirit of Lord of the Rings, not a mage throwing fireballs and lightning bolts.

- **Everyone chosen can use it.** All players can use magic once claimed by a god; the god decides which magic they can take. Every character can fight and craft without it, so magic is a mysterious bonus, not a tool for everyday use. Reverse-engineered relics of old are the exception that everyday adventurers use, such as the warp bag every adventurer carries their belongings in, and the rarer warp bracelet that opens a personal warp space to call home.
- **It powers the greatest machines.** Even the greatest technomancers rely on the mysterious force of one of the Gods to power their contraptions.
- **Ordinary machines are separate.** Turrets, sentries and everyday contraptions run without any god's power.
- **Forbidden to most.** Outside the chosen, magic is outlawed among the "good" races.

### Hrimfolk

A hardy race that settled the north and grew used to the cold. They have become more barbaric and primal in their ways, and are sturdier than all other races. Sailors, warriors, great laborers and great hunters.

### Aurelans

An ingenious race on the eastern islands, where farmland is abundant. They built a feudal system and stand at the frontier of humanity.

### Ilvari

A secretive race that loved the magic they lost, and developed technology to replace it. As their magic waned, so did their numbers. Now a small society of individuals devoted to remaking magic with technology, marked with blue ink to show their support for magic. They are travelers, met in the world through quests. (Comparable in spirit to elves.)

### Ferrin

A brutal but valorous race on the Slag Isles, a land of volcanoes and rolling hills. Centuries of mining made them short. They wield dreadnoughts, gunpowder, artillery and war golems. (Comparable in spirit to dwarves.)

### Zarhim

Nomads of the deserts, comparable to Skyrim's Redguards. Good tamers and survivors, armed with traditional weapons: swords, bows and crossbows. Naturally agile, they hold out better than any other race in the harshest environments.

### Ruadhan

A tall race with red or orange hair, living in hills and forests with good access to rare metals; a mix of Bannerlord's Battanians and Skyrim's Bretons. Good traders and fierce warriors. Their main craft is animal taming, for war and for trade.

### Wayfolk

The mixed races: travelers and commoners with no specialty, but the most numerous people in the world. They live under the God of travels.

### The Forsaken

The people of the 14th God: corrupted, without a moral compass, guided by a deep magic. Some were touched by the magic of the 13th God's sacrifice, which lifted their corruption and made them playable. The freed are called the Penitent. They are the most magically gifted of all, but suffer for their past and live as flagellants.

### Briarkin, the wild men (not playable)

Men who became wild and one with nature, like Skyrim's Forsworn. They serve as a bandit race.

### The Lessborn, the cursed (not playable)

Men cursed by their own pursuits: they sacrificed animals to the gods and became more like animals themselves. Brutes with beast-like features, comparable to orcs and trolls, made up of several subgroups. They are the children of the lesser gods, never favorites, and cannot form kingdoms; tribes are the most they manage. In the story, the great evil can unite them into armies.

### The Gods (not playable)

The Gods themselves are a race of higher power.

## Regions and cities (draft proposal)

The world is a map of continents and isles, like World of Warcraft's. When the 1st God split Erde, the land broke into continents that drifted apart across the seas; their coasts have long since weathered into natural shapes, though old cliffs and chasms still show where the land tore. At the heart of the main continent stands the **city of the 1st God, Aldrath**, the greatest city of the third age. Its inner walls have been sealed by magic for so long that no one alive has seen inside. At the edges of the known world lies the **Sundering**, where Erde was broken: no man's land, where horror stories are born, sailors go missing and the Fog is strongest. Each living god sits at the heart of a city, and each city has a warp at its centre. Names are working names.

### Life and the Fog

The Fog does not only breed monsters: it weakens life itself. The farther a place lies from Aldrath, the thinner its harvests, the sicklier its herds, the shorter its growing seasons and the harder its winters. Everyone believes this is **Aldrath's power**: the 1st God's sealed city radiates a protection that holds the Fog back, strongest at the centre and fading with distance, and each lesser god can only keep a smaller circle clear around their own city. So the centre is where life is easiest, and people have drifted toward it for generations.

- **Aldrath is the biggest city by far:** a crowded refuge and melting pot where every people has a quarter, the trading hub of the world, as Stormwind or Orgrimmar are in their worlds.
- **Each people still keeps a hometown,** its capital and the seat of its god, but these are smaller, fortified and specialised. How each one fares depends on its people (below).
- **Trade holds the world together:** grain and food flow outward from the fertile centre; ores, furs, timber, spices, beasts and relics flow inward. No people can live alone, which is why the crafts interlock.
- **The hidden truth:** the protection is real, but it also keeps the peoples gathered and dependent on Aldrath, exactly where he wants them. Players learn this late.

**The start: the fall of Farhallow.** Players begin as freefolk in **Farhallow** *(working name)*, a fishing and farming village on the main continent's far coast, outside any god's circle: home to freefolk of every people who could not afford to move inward. The Fog takes it on the first evening of the game.

1. **Calm before:** a late afternoon in the village, colours already a little faded so far from Aldrath. Small chores teach moving, looking, talking and picking things up.
2. **The Fog rolls in:** at dusk the bell rings, colour drains from the world and things move in the mist.
3. **The first weapon:** in the panic the player grabs a weapon (an old blade from above a door, a woodsman's hatchet, a fisher's spear), picks it up, opens their equipment and equips it: the first item and the first weapon of their journey.
4. **The first fight:** a Fog-warped animal (a wolf, a boar, a dog), fought with that weapon.
5. **The hard moment:** a neighbour, half-changed by the Fog, grey-veined and moving wrongly: the grimdark beneath the surface, shown once, early.
6. **Something larger:** a huge shape in the Fog tears down the village gate. It is never fought; everyone runs.
7. **The escape:** through fires and fleeing survivors to the harbour and the last ship out.
8. **The crossing:** through the night the Fog thins and colour returns; at dawn Aldrath's glow rises on the horizon.
9. **Arrival at Hollin's Cross,** the starter port, where the world opens; Aldrath's outer city stands about two kilometres to its north.

**Merrith's touch.** If the player falls during the prologue, the 13th God's lingering magic lifts them up again with a soft glow: tutorial safety that is also the first sign that they are one of the chosen.

**What it means:** a village falling so far inward is a sign that Aldrath's protection is weakening, the first hint of what the story will reveal. Later, each people may get its own opening in its own homeland, all ending on a ship to Hollin's Cross *(proposal)*. Like Riverwood, Whiterun and Falkreath, places sit a real journey apart, and quests lead onward without hand-holding.

**The three moons.** Three moons cross the night sky on their own paths, so they rise and set at different times and change position from night to night: a large pale moon, a small violet moon, and a broken moon split by a jagged crack, its fragments drifting beside it. The three moons are gods themselves, who chose to seclude themselves from the world. The gods behind them are forgotten; only the names people gave the moons remain. The large pale moon is barren, devoid of everything. The small violet moon is a god's own self and the home of his people, a mysterious race he placed there; this is a secret for now, and reaching it belongs to a future expansion. The broken moon is a consequence of the first war: it was struck by a stray attack from one of the beings of the first age, whose actions brought cataclysm upon the world (which being does not matter), and remains broken as a permanent reminder that some forces in the world can move more than just mountains, or so the legends say.

**Aldrath's city and where the journey begins.** The outer city is alive, the greatest city of the third age, reachable by ship and by warp. It is about 600 metres across, the trading hub and main town of the world, with a quarter for each people around a grand plaza where statues of the twelve face the sealed walls. Aldrath sealed the inner city himself, as a safe haven for the weaker gods, at least in theory. Every player arrives as one of the freefolk of their people, a survivor of Farhallow, not yet claimed by any god. Being claimed by a god later in the journey decides which magic a player can take; there are no classes.

**The Forsaken on the mainland.** The Forsaken have reached the mainlands, bringing portions of the Fog with them: lighter and manageable, unlike the Sundering. Some ports still stand in these lands. The wilds there are more untamed than corrupted, because magic may still linger.

**Places without a living people**

- **The shattered capital of the first age**: the endgame. Where it lies, perhaps sunk in the Sundering, is still open.
- **Animari ruins**: machine cities of the lost technomancers, in the mountain spine *(proposal)*.
- **Vael's ruins**: the ancient civilization that built the warps, with broken warps that no longer open *(proposal)*.
- **Verdane's groves**: shrines of nature magic scattered across every region, since she taught all peoples *(proposal)*.
- **The Ilvari** have no city; they travel, with small enclaves and caravans.

**Scale:** the world stays within the 20 km budget for now, spread across the continents and isles: Aldrath's heartland is the largest region, and each hometown's domain spans a few kilometres. Following the plan to build a playable world first, launch could start with Aldrath's outer city and one neighbouring domain, then open further domains in later updates *(proposal)*.

**Open:** who lives in Aldrath's outer city and how it is reached; which peoples share which lands; where the Forsaken came from and where the shattered capital lies; which smaller lands are saved for future releases.
