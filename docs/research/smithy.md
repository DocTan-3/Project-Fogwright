# Research: a working village smithy

Before building an interior, find out what the real thing has and how it is laid out. This is the reference for
Farhallow's smithy (the workshop type, prologue beat B) and any later smithy.

## What a smithy has

| Item | What it is, where it goes | Sources |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Forge** (hearth) | A waist-high hearth built on a stone base with a brick or stone box, its back against a wall so the **hood and chimney** rise behind it; the working sides stay open. The fire sits in a fire pot fed with air from below or the back. | NJSOC lesson; Artisan Blacksmith; Wikipedia "Forge" |
| **Great bellows** | Large double bellows worked by a lever or rocker pole, hung from the rafters or set beside or behind the forge and chimney, their nozzle into the fire; worked by an apprentice or family member. | I Forge Iron; Fanshawe Pioneer Village; Stagecoach Inn Museum |
| **Anvil on its stump** | A wooden block base; set close to the forge (a turn and a step from the fire) at knuckle height so the hammer strikes square; the horn to the smith's off hand. | NJSOC lesson; Clio "The Barn" |
| **Slack tub** | A tub or half barrel of water by the forge and anvil, for quenching and tempering, and for damping the fire. | Appropedia; I Forge Iron; Wikipedia "Forge" |
| **Fuel** | Charcoal or coal in a bin or heap beside the forge. | Wikipedia "Forge" |
| **Tongs** | Many pairs (Fort Vancouver listed 40), racked at the forge's edge within reach. | NPS Fort Vancouver; NPS blacksmith terms |
| **Hammers, punches, chisels, fullers** | On a rack or in holes by the anvil. | NPS blacksmith terms |
| **Swage block** | A heavy iron block with holes and grooves of many shapes, on its own stand. | NPS blacksmith terms |
| **Mandrel** | A cone of iron for rings and hoops. | Clio "The Barn" |
| **Workbench and vise** | Benches with leg or post vises for clamping work to file, cut and bend (Fort Vancouver: three bench vises). | NPS Fort Vancouver; NPS blacksmith terms |
| **Grindstone** | Sharpening; often by the door for light. | Clio "The Barn" |
| **Stock and tool racks** | Bars and rods of iron stock stood or racked by length. | Clio "The Barn" |
| **Locked chest** | For the smith's most valued tools. | Clio "The Barn" |
| **Shoeing tools, nails** | Where the smith also shoes horses. | NPS Fort Vancouver |

## The building

- **Large front doors** for horses and carts; **few windows**, because dim light lets the smith judge the colour, and so
  the heat, of hot iron. (Fanshawe Pioneer Village)
- **Dirt floor**, against sparks. (Fanshawe Pioneer Village)
- The forge, anvil and slack tub form a tight working triangle of a step or two; benches, the grindstone and racks go
  round the walls; stock and finished work by the door or in the store.

## For Farhallow's smith (Old Tobin)

A small coastal village smith: boat fittings, hooks, chain, nails, tools, the odd horseshoe for the farm horse, and the
blade above the door (his father's, never sold). So: one forge and bellows, one anvil, a slack tub, a coal bin, tong and
hammer racks, a swage block, a bench and vise, a grindstone at the door, iron stock and finished boat fittings (chain,
hooks, a small anchor) in the store, a locked chest, a nail keg. Earth floor, dim, the fire the main light.
