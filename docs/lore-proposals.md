# Lore proposals: filling in the world

2026-11-28 · drafted for Doc Tan

A big pass on the lore, built on everything in [lore.md](lore.md) and kept true to it. Nothing here is decided: every
section is a **proposal** to keep, change or throw away. Once something is chosen it moves into lore.md and this file
loses it. Names are working names, like everywhere else.

What this covers: the names still missing, why the gods fade, what the Fog really is, the truth about Aldrath, the lost
peoples, the factions, daily faith, the calendar and money, the creatures of the Fog, the main story in acts, and an
answer to every open question in lore.md.

**The one idea that ties it together:** magic is like water. When a chosen person uses magic, it flows back to the god
who claimed them, the way rain returns to the sea. That return is what keeps a god alive. Magic that has no god to go
back to does not vanish: it pools, sours and becomes the Fog. Almost every mystery below follows from that one rule.

## Decisions so far

Answered on 2026-11-28 and merged into lore.md: **kept** the Return (1), Aldrath's well (2), the inner city as a larder
(3), the Fog remembers (4), Merrith spread through the Fog (5) and the year 312 TA (7). **Dropped:** players being
claimed by Aldrath (6); he claims no one (below). **Reworked:** the Forsaken's origin (8) is now the Greyed, remade (in lore.md).
Second round, also merged into lore.md: the Concord and Aumenor; Aldrath's motive ("one Rule, one God"); the 14th
as his second body; Galeth alive and held in the Godless Reach; the Animari asleep in their machines; Vael **imprisoned
by Aldrath** in a broken warp; the Sennari; the Vigil **marches as one** (no split); the pusher lamps; the Ordinal and
Censors; the calendar; **one coin only**; the moons are **looks only**; the five kinds of Fog creature. The endgame is
left open. Sections below that disagree with these are being kept only as history of the idea; lore.md wins.

**Aldrath claims no one.** The Ordinal teaches that the Rule is above choosing favourites: Aldrath gives his grace to the
whole world through his circle, not to a few. It sounds generous. It is also one more clue, since a god who claims no
chosen gets no Return from them, and yet he alone never fades.

## The truth of the world, in order

The real history, as the story will finally reveal it. Players learn it piece by piece, never all at once.

### The first age: the Concord

One empire of men ruled all of Erde from Aumenor, and many gods blessed it on two conditions: men must never fight one
another, and men must never think themselves above the gods. In return the gods gave grace freely. Every man and woman
could touch magic a little, and every drop of it flowed back to the gods. That is **the Return**, and it was the
Concord's quiet heart: the people were the gods' rivers, and the gods were the people's sea.

There were dozens of gods then, great and small. The moons were three of them, who grew tired of the world and drew
themselves up into the sky, taking their light and their silence with them. Nobody remembers their names; their peoples
named the moons instead.

**The corruption.** The 1st God, Aldrath, saw something the others did not: the Return could be bent. A god who stood
where all the rivers met would drink from all of them. He did not want to rule the gods. He wanted to be the only one,
"one Rule, one God". He whispered to the ambitious among the gods, and they whispered to their peoples, and men went to
war for them. Both rules broke at once.

**The Splitting.** When the war reached Aumenor itself, Aldrath "ended" it. He broke Erde apart with a single working
so great that a stray piece of it struck the third moon and cracked it, and Aumenor fell into the tear. The other gods
saw a hero. He punished the corrupt gods he had stirred up, confining them together in the Godless Reach, and sent every
god with their people to their own land. Each people was changed by its god into what we see now. A new peace was born.

**What it really did:** the Splitting scattered the peoples so no other god could ever gather the Return as Aumenor once
had, and it put Aldrath's own land at the centre of everything.

### The second age: the Ban

The second age lasted about a thousand years. The peoples grew clever and proud, built steam and brass, and fought.

**The Ban.** Aldrath told the gods that magic in the hands of men was what had corrupted the first age, and that the
only safe magic was magic held by a few chosen. The gods agreed, out of guilt. Magic was outlawed among the "good"
peoples; each god claimed only a handful of chosen. The Return became a trickle, and the gods began to fade slowly,
over centuries, without understanding why. Aldrath alone did not fade, because the trickle that remained flowed through
his land on its way anywhere.

**The Fog.** Magic used illegally, by hedge-witches, desperate soldiers, cults and dying men, still flowed out of them,
but with no god to claim it, it had nowhere to go. It mixed with the smoke and fire of the endless wars, pooled in the
low places and soured. That is the Fog: magic without a home. It does not think, but it remembers. It takes the
shapes of what it touched, which is why a Fog wolf has the wrong number of eyes and a Fog-touched neighbour still hums the
song she was singing.

### The third age: the war against the Fog

**Merrith's sacrifice.** The Fog grew until it covered most of the world. Merrith the Merciful, the 13th God and the
kindest, did the only thing a god could do with it: she gave herself to it. She poured her whole being into the Fog
and thinned it for three hundred years. That night began the third age.

But a god does not end like a candle. Merrith is spread through the Fog like a thread through cloth. Her thread is the
mysterious force that guides the chosen to their gods; it is why players feel a pull toward places, why a fallen player
rises in the prologue, and why some Forsaken woke up from their corruption. She is not dead. She is everywhere and very
thin.

**Aldrath's protection.** The twelve sit in their cities, each keeping a small circle clear. Aldrath's circle is
enormous, and life is easiest near him. Everyone believes his sealed city radiates protection. The truth: his sealed city
is a well. It pulls magic in from the whole world. Near the centre that pull drags the Fog away, so the protection is
real. But everything it pulls goes into him, and the farther a land lies from the centre, the more of its life has been
drawn away over the centuries. The thin harvests at the edges are not the Fog's doing alone. They are his.

**The inner city.** Aldrath sealed his inner city as "a safe haven for the weaker gods". Over the centuries, gods too
faded to hold their own circle were brought inside, with great ceremony, "to rest in the Rule". None came out. The inner
city is a larder. **(proposal: nearly empty, a few faded gods still alive and imprisoned, which the players can free in
the endgame.)**

**The 14th.** In the Godless Reach, the corrupt gods kept using their magic, and with no Return they withered. Aldrath went
there in secret, in a second body made of his own pulled-in power, and devoured them one by one. That second body is the
14th. To the twelve he still sits behind his walls. To the Godless Reach he is the only god. He now has enough power that
he no longer needs the twelve alive. He needs them to fade a little faster, and he needs their peoples gathered close to
the centre, where the well can drink them when the time comes.

**The fall of Galeth.** Galeth the Vigilant, god of protection, stands at the Last Bastion facing the Sundering. He has
started to suspect where the drawn magic goes, because his circle shrinks from the wrong side. During the story he is
"devoured by the Fog". In truth the 14th takes him. **(proposal: Galeth is not dead either; he is held in the
Godless Reach, and the Vigil's search for him is the road to the truth.)**

### Why Aldrath does it

Not cruelty for its own sake. Aldrath is the god of the Rule, and he believes the first age fell because there were too
many wills in the world. One god, one law, one Return: in his eyes that is order, and order is mercy. He sees the twelve
as a failed experiment and the peoples as rivers to be straightened. That makes him a villain with a reason players can
understand.

## The lost peoples

### The Animari, people of Oriel

Oriel the Lucid gave the Animari understanding so deep that they could move their minds into the things they understood:
first animals, then machines. They lived in **machine cities** in the mountain spine of the north, where every door and
pump was something a mind could ride. Technomancy was their art.

**What happened to them:** in the second age the Ban fell hardest on the Animari, because their whole life was magic.
Most refused to give it up. Some say they were wiped out by a crusade of the other peoples; the truth is stranger. When
the Ban cut the Return, the Animari moved their minds into their machines one last time and never came back. **Their
cities are not empty: the minds are still in the machines, asleep or mad.** That makes Animari ruins the best dungeons in
the game: the walls themselves can wake up.

**Oriel** fades slowest of the lost gods because a trickle still returns from the sleeping minds. She can still claim
chosen. Her chosen are the only ones who can wake an Animari machine without it going mad.

**Technomancers today** are outlaws who copy the old art crudely from Animari relics. People call them **cogwitches**.

### The Sennari, people of Vael

Vael's people, the Sennari or Doorfolk, built the **warps** in the first age: gates that open a path between two places.
After the Splitting the warps were what held the scattered world together, and the great cities grew round them.

**What happened to them:** early in the second age, Vael stepped through a warp by accident into the place where Aldrath
keeps his plans (a hidden hall under the inner city, **the Undercroft of the Rule**). He saw everything and understood
that if Aldrath knew he had seen, he and his people would die. So he hid: he faked his own death, closed every Sennari
warp he could, and left his people behind without a word. Without their god the Sennari faded within two generations.
Their last writings curse him. The Wayfolk still say "gone like Vael" for a man who walks out on his family.

**Where Vael is now (decided):** imprisoned. After years in hiding, Aldrath found him and locked him in a broken warp.
He is the one being alive who knows the truth, and freeing him is the story's turning point (Act III).

**Broken warps:** Sennari ruins hold warps that no longer open. A Warpwalker can wake some of them for a moment,
which is how players reach places no ship can.

### The Lantern Vigil, the order of Galeth

Galeth never had a people of his own. He took the sworn instead: men and women of any people who gave up their name to
stand between the world and what would harm it. They are the **Lantern Vigil**, a crusading order.

- **The Lantern:** every Vigil knight carries a flame lit from **the First Flame** in the Last Bastion, which Galeth
  himself lit. A Vigil flame thins the Fog around it. The adventurer's lantern every player carries is a humble copy:
  the Vigil sells "lit wicks" from its chapter houses, and that is the most common way ordinary people meet the order.
- **The pusher lamps** that light the streets of the cities are a Vigil and Ferrin invention: copper coils wound and
  blessed in the chapter houses, which hum at a pitch the Fog shies away from. When Galeth falls, the lamps flicker
  across the world for one night: the first sign every player sees that something is badly wrong.
- **Ranks:** Wick (novice), Lamplighter, Warden-Knight, Flamekeeper, and the Lord-Commander at the Bastion.
- **After Galeth falls,** the whole Vigil marches into the Fog to find him, as one (decided).

### Verdane's groves

Verdane the Open-Handed chose no people. She taught every people a little of the living world: when to plant, how
weather turns, how to coax growth. Her shrines, the **groves**, stand in every region. Because she has no people, almost
nothing should return to her, and she should have faded first. She has not. The Ordinal never counted her weather-lore
and growing charms as magic ("folk-craft", it calls them), so farmers still use a little of it every season, and every
little use returns to her. **The groves are where grace is closest to the old Return, and they are the clue that
unravels the whole truth:** an Ilvari scholar notices that the one god whose magic ordinary people still use is the
one god fading slowest.

### The Briarkin and the Lessborn

- **Briarkin** are not just bandits. They are freefolk who refused the Ban outright and still use wild, godless magic in
  the woods. Every spell they cast feeds the Fog, so the land round their camps is greyer and stranger, and their
  shamans are often half-changed. They hate the gods for "rationing the rain". Some of what they say is true.
- **Lessborn** are the peoples of the lesser gods of the first age, who sacrificed beasts to feed their gods when the
  Return was not enough. Their gods faded anyway, and the peoples kept the beast-shapes. Subgroups (proposal):
  **Tusked** (boar-like, hill tribes), **Horned** (goat and ram, mountains), **Hide-backs** (bear, the northern forests),
  **Crookjaws** (hyena-like, the Sere's fringes). They pray to gods who no longer answer, which makes them easy for the
  14th to unite: he answers.

## Faith and power in daily life

### The Ordinal and the Censors

Aldrath's faith is **the Ordinal**, the official church of the Rule. It runs the plaza of the twelve, the courts of
Aldrath's outer city and the licences for the chosen.

- **Ordinants** are its priests: judges, record keepers and preachers of order.
- **The Censure** is its arm that enforces the Ban. Its agents, **Censors**, hunt illegal magic, cogwitches and
  Briarkin. They wear grey coats and carry **sounding bells**: small brass bells that ring on their own near unlicensed
  magic.
- **The Writ:** when a god claims a player, the Ordinal issues a **Writ of Grace**, a sealed licence that lets them use
  magic openly. Without it, a chosen is just another witch. (A mechanic: see mechanics-proposals.md.)
- The Ordinal is not evil. Most Ordinants believe in order and mercy. Only a few at the top serve the truth knowingly,
  which keeps the reveal from turning every priest into a villain.

### How the other gods are worshipped

- Every hometown has its **god's seat**: the god lives there, and on high days is seen.
- Small shrines everywhere: Hollin's waystones, Verdane's groves, Merrith's candle shrines (she has no seat, so her
  shrines are kept by volunteers like Farhallow's shrine keeper, Wenna Rook).
- Offerings are things of use, not gold: bread to Maelis, a tool to Ostrak, a song to Seraveth, water to Khesra.
- **Prayer does not feed a god.** Only magic returns. People do not know this, which is part of the tragedy: whole
  peoples are praying their hearts out to gods who are starving.

### The plaza of the twelve

The statues of the twelve gods in Aldrath's plaza face the sealed walls. **(proposal)** There are thirteen plinths; the
thirteenth, for Merrith, is empty, and people leave candles on it. Late in the story a fourteenth plinth is found under
the paving.

## Time, money and the sky

### The calendar (proposal)

- **Years** are counted from Merrith's sacrifice: the game begins in **312 TA** (Third Age).
- **Twelve months** of 30 days, one named for each of the twelve, plus **the Mourning**, five days at the year's end that
  belong to Merrith. Nobody works or fights during the Mourning; even raiders keep it. (A yearly world event.)
- **Weeks** of six days, so every month is five weeks.
- **Festivals:** the Feast of Roads (Hollin's month, the Wayfolk), the Long Night (Hrothvar's, a midwinter vigil), the
  Charter Days (Maelis's, Aurelan oaths renewed), Ashwake (Ostrak's, forges relit), the Wellnight (Khesra's, water
  shared), the Beast Fair (Brannoch's, the great Ruadhan market), the Lamplighting (Galeth's, every lamp relit from a
  Vigil flame).

## The Fog and what lives in it

The Fog is soured, godless magic. It remembers what it touches and copies it badly.

| Kind | What it is | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Fogtouched** | Animals the Fog has seeped into: still themselves, but wrong | Grey wolves with too many eyes, boars with bone growing through the hide, gulls that fly in silence |
| **Greyed** | People taken slowly by the Fog: grey veins, then grey eyes, then they move like the Fog moves | The half-changed neighbour in Farhallow; whole Greyed villages on the far coasts, still "living" their last day |
| **Wrought** | Big shapes the Fog builds from memories of many things: a mass that is part beast, part wall, part ship | The huge shape tearing down Farhallow's gate |
| **Echoes** | The Fog's memory of the dead or of the first age, replaying where they happened | A battle that fights itself every night on an old field; Sennari walking through warps that no longer open |
| **Remnants** | In the Sundering: the Fog's memory of the first-age beings who split the world. Endgame bosses | (to design with the endgame) |

**Greying (the corruption):** five stages, so players and settlers can see it coming: a chill, grey veins, grey eyes,
the "tune" (hearing a song in the Fog), and the turn. Up to the tune an Herbalist's remedy or a god's grace can bring a
person back. After the turn, nothing can, except, rumour says, Merrith.

**Fog shards** are the Fog's memory gone solid: grey crystals left when a Fog creature dies. They are full of soured
magic. Caster foci burn them, inventions use them, and the Ordinal buys them up "to be destroyed". (Where they actually go
is one more clue: the Ordinal ships them all to the inner city.)

## The main story, in acts (proposal)

Built so launch can ship with the Prologue and Act I, and each update adds an act. Every act ends on a place the players
can now reach.

### Prologue: The fall of Farhallow (as in lore.md)

Ends at Hollin's Cross at dawn. The player is one of the freefolk, unclaimed, with Merrith's thread pulling at them.

### Act I: The Chosen

- **The pull.** Merrith's thread leads the player to a Merrith candle shrine in Hollin's Cross, where the shrine keeper
  (a Penitent, which shocks people) explains what the pull means: a god is looking for them.
- **The Writ.** In Aldrath's outer city the Ordinal tests and registers them. They meet the Censure for the first
  time: polite, cold, watching.
- **The seeking.** The player chooses which god to seek and travels to that god's seat (or a grove for Verdane). Each god has a **claiming trial** that shows what that god values. This is the first long journey.
- **Claimed.** Magic opens; the warps open to them.
- **The first sign.** On the night after the claiming, every pusher lamp in the world flickers at once. Nobody
  explains it.
- Ends with access to the warp network and the first hometown.

### Act II: The Fading

- Colour retreats a little from one hometown (it differs each playthrough: the god the players' group neglected).
- **The fall of Galeth.** The Last Bastion's First Flame goes out. Galeth is gone. The Vigil marches into the Fog;
  players can join the march.
- In the Fog they find not Galeth's remains but **a drag trail**: a path where the Fog itself was pulled toward the
  south-west. Toward the Godless Reach, everyone assumes.
- An Ilvari archivist, **Ithel Varr** (working name), shows the players the Verdane clue: the one god ordinary people
  still use is the one not fading. She is arrested by the Censure the next day.
- Ends with the Sundering coast and the first Sennari ruins.

### Act III: The Empty Lands

- The expedition to the Godless Reach. There are no evil gods: their seats are empty, their temples gnawed.
- One will rules the Forsaken now: the 14th. The Lessborn tribes march under him for the first time in history.
- **Vael freed.** In a broken Sennari warp the players find where Aldrath locked Vael away, and free him. Tired and
  ashamed, he tells them what he saw in the Undercroft of the Rule. He cannot prove it.
- Players must decide whom to tell, knowing the Censure will call it heresy.
- Ends with Vael's broken warps, which lead under Aldrath.

### Act IV: The Sealed City

- Into the Undercroft through Vael's warp. The plans, the larder, the faded gods held in the dark.
- **The reveal:** Aldrath and the 14th are one.
- **The choice for everyone:** tell the world, knowing the Ordinal will call it heresy, or keep the secret and work
  against him from inside. **(proposal: this splits endings, not servers.)**
- The remaining gods are told. Some refuse to believe it.
- Ends with the road to Aumenor.

### Endgame: The Crown of Erde (left open; an early idea)

- Aumenor, sunk in the Sundering, where the Concord fell and the Splitting began. The Remnants guard it.
- At its heart is **the Root**: the place where all the Return of the first age met. Whoever holds it holds the
  world's magic. Aldrath has always wanted to come back here.
- **Merrith:** if enough of the Fog has been cleared and enough Greyed brought back, her thread is thick enough to
  gather. The best ending lets her return as the Return itself: magic flowing to every god again, as in the first age,
  and the Ban broken. (This makes players' long work against the Fog, across the whole server, matter to the ending.)

## Who suspects the truth (answer to an open question)

1. **Vael**, who knows, and hides.
2. **The Hrimfolk skalds**, who sing that "the centre is too warm" without knowing why.
3. **Galeth**, who suspects and is taken for it.
4. **Ithel Varr** and the Ilvari Archive, who find the Verdane clue.
5. **The Penitent**, who hear the One Below speak in Aldrath's liturgy, and are not believed because of what they were.
6. **One Ordinant**: an old judge of the Ordinal, **Prelate Corwen Ashe** (working name), who has noticed that the
   Fog shards go into the inner city and nothing comes out. He becomes the players' secret ally in Aldrath.

## The main continent: places and history (adopted for now, 2026-11-28)

A Skyrim-style spread of named places for the playable main continent (about 20 km across), each with its history.
All names are working names. Map: `docs/references/main-continent-map.png`.

### A history of the land

| When | What happened here |
| --- | --- |
| **First age** | The Concord rules from Aumenor. On this land it builds **Ostrevan**, a great city on the north-east coast, and the **Concord road** that runs from the river meeting (where Aldrath now stands) to Ostrevan in one direction and to the south-west coast in the other. Concord governors are buried in the **Kingsbarrows**. The empire holds **Crownmere**, the Aldwater's source, sacred as the first gift of the gods. |
| **The split of Erde** | The head of the gods tears the continent apart. The tear runs through Ostrevan: the **Scar** leaves half the city on the clifftop and drops half into the chasm. The Brack starts to pour over the new cliff as the **Long Fall**. |
| **Second age, early** | Aldrath's city rises at the meeting of the Aldwater and the Stillwater. The Ban on magic is proclaimed; outlaw hedge-witches are hanged on **Hangman's Rise** within sight of the city. At **Brannoch's Leap**, the Ruadhan say, their god tamed the first horse for his new people. |
| **Second age, the Ford Wars** | The Ruadhan of the Redwold and the heartland's lords fight for a century over the fords of the Rede. The **Old Front** is dug: trenches, gun forts (Gunhill, Fort Cinder), the **Trenchworks** tunnels. A war dam is blown to drown an army, making **the Sunken Field** and the Spill. The **Beacon Line** of signal towers is built along the east to warn of raids by sea. Unhallowed magic used in these wars feeds the young Fog. |
| **0 TA** | Merrith's sacrifice ends the second age. That night **Stillmere** turns mirror-still, and no Fog has settled on it since. |
| **4 TA** | Hollin grants the first farm of the new age, **Hearthstead**, to Wayfolk settlers; the Downs are farmed from it. |
| **12 TA** | Pilgrims who saw Stillmere refuse the Fog found **Mere's End** on its north shore. |
| **About 40 TA** | The **Mourning Abbey** is built on the south-east coast to keep Merrith's memory; the Mourning days are first kept there. |
| **About 100 TA** | The Lantern Vigil lays the **lantern roads**, mostly along the old Concord road; **Lampwatch** is founded to wind coils for the east road. **Vespers** grows round the Ordinal's seminary. |
| **About 180 TA** | Freefolk settle the far coast because the land is free: Farhallow, Greyford, Morrow, Coldwell and others. |
| **About 250 TA** | Deserters burn Fort Cinder rather than give it up and become **the Unlit**, who live by robbing the lantern roads. |
| **287 TA** | The Fog takes the Mourning Abbey. Its bells still ring at dusk with no one to ring them. |
| **309 TA** | **Greyford** falls; its people are seen walking the Greyrun's banks, grey-veined. |
| **312 TA** | The game begins: **Farhallow** falls in the prologue. Coldwell is now the last freehold standing on the coast. |

### Land and water

| Feature | Where | History and what it is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The Kingsteeth** | The north | The main range, 500 to 800 m. Named in the first age for the Concord's kings, who were crowned facing them. Highest peak **Old Crown** |
| **The Redwold hills** | The west | Forested hills of the Ruadhan, sickening at their outer edge |
| **The Downs** | South of Aldrath | Rolling farm hills, farmed since Hearthstead (4 TA); the safest country in the world |
| **The Scar** | The north-east coast | The cliff and chasm left by the split of Erde, running through Ostrevan |
| **The Weeping Fen** | The Greying Coast | A grey salt marsh where the Fog pools round Farhallow's bay |
| **Crownmere** | Below Old Crown | A glacial tarn, source of the Aldwater, ringed by Concord carvings |
| **Stillmere** | East of Aldrath | Where Merrith's thread runs thickest since 0 TA. The Fog never settles on it; Greyed people standing in it remember their names for a moment; pilgrims come during the Mourning. A clue on the story trail: Aldrath's well draws from everywhere but here |
| **The Sunken Field** | The Old Front | The lake made when a war dam was blown in the Ford Wars. Gun towers and war machines stand under the water; on still days Echoes march across it |
| **The Aldwater** | Crownmere to the sea at Hollin's Cross | The great river; mills, waterwheels, barges. Aldrath stands where it meets the Stillwater |
| **The Stillwater** | Stillmere west into the Aldwater | A slow, clear stream; Aldrath's fountains draw on it |
| **The Rede** | The Redwold east into the Aldwater | Its fords were fought over for a century |
| **The Greyrun** | Stillmere south-east to the Weeping Fen | Clear near the lake, grey toward the coast: the Fog follows water. The east lantern road runs beside it |
| **The Brack** | Eastern Kingsteeth to the Scar | Falls into the chasm as **the Long Fall**, misting Ostrevan's lower half |
| **Thornwater** | The Redwold south to the coast | The Ruadhan float timber down it |
| **The Spill** | The Sunken Field into the Rede | Born when the dam broke; still runs with rust |
